In the age of AI there is endless hype all around. Online on places like LinkedIn. In person at meetups and conferences. At work. Most anyone who works in tech who I've interacted with recently will talk your ear off about how they're excited about AI and using it.

What I have noticed though is many conversations are surface-y and generic LinkedIn post style things. I'm an engineer and want to know exactly _what_ you're doing and _how_ you're doing it. Mostly so we lift eachother up by learning from eachother's experience.

Through these conversations in 2026, I've noticed a high level trend of three different types of builders. Here they are starting with the most common to least common from my experience.

## 1. The builder who _uses AI to build_

This is by far the most common profile I come across. These are folks who use AI coding assistants such as codex or claude to plan and write code. There are also some traditionally non-engineers using the same tools for their work. Think folks like product managers, people in marketing, and customer service. Sometimes they even have these tools write code while not fully understanding it. My opinion: this is fine as long as it is a POC and not shipped to prod without an engineer who knows what they're doing either reviewing or rebuilding it.

## 2. The builder who _builds with AI_

This group is less common, but they do exist! These are often engineers who are building agents. Think things like software factories to build software, specific purpose agents, of they're using LLM output as part of their app. In addition they might be experimenting with setting up and running local models for their personal use. I think the big change in this group against group 1 is that the things that they build are deployed to the cloud, so their laptops not going to sleep is not part of the equation.

## 3. The builder who _is building AI_

This group is very much the smalled from my experience. These are folks who are building and training models. Think AI researchers or maybe engineers at Anthropic or OpenAI building tools around their proprietary models used by millions of people.

## Prediction

Ok bold prediction time! Keep in mind this post was written in 2026 and I'm fine with this not aging well.

Right now at the time of me writing this, I beleive that with the hype of "AI taking engineering jobs" from humans has a little bit of substance. I believe that the builders who fall into category 2 and 3 will be fine. Over time category 1 builders will be replace with whatever the category 2 builders build.

## Closing

There's lots of noise and hype out there about usage of AI. Especially on LinkedIn where folks are fighting to get a job in a tight market...but be able to tell the signal from the noise. There are tons of ways one could be using AI. Some are more involved than others.
